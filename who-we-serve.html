Small US exporters that ship first and get paid later.

We will work with one kind of company, deliberately. Narrow focus is what lets a small policy get real attention.

The exporter we are built for

You make or supply something, you sell it to companies outside the United States, and you extend them credit because that is how your market works. Your foreign receivables are probably your largest unprotected asset.

Size

Fewer than 500 employees, the federal definition of a small business. Most of our clients will be far smaller. There is no minimum export volume to talk to us.

What you sell

Manufactured goods, components, equipment, materials and related services. The product should be substantially made in the United States; we will check this with you early because it affects eligibility.

Who buys it

Foreign commercial companies: distributors, OEMs, contractors, processors, retailers. Sales to foreign governments and state-owned buyers can also be covered, with some differences we will explain.

How you get paid

On terms. Net 30, net 60, net 90, documents against acceptance, or anything else where goods leave before money arrives. If you already require letters of credit or payment in advance from every buyer, you may not need us, though you may be losing sales to competitors who offer terms.

Where you ship

Anywhere coverage is available. Canada, Mexico, Western Europe, East Asia, Latin America and the Middle East are the most common destinations for the companies we are talking to. Country eligibility changes; we will check each buyer's country at application.

A quick fit check

These are the questions we will ask on a first call. If you can answer yes to most of the left column, it is worth a conversation.

Likely a good fit

  • You sell to foreign companies and invoice them on terms.
  • Foreign sales are a meaningful share of revenue, or you want them to be.
  • You have been asked for terms by a buyer you had to turn down.
  • You have had a slow-paying or non-paying foreign customer and absorbed it.
  • Your bank discounts or excludes foreign receivables from your borrowing base.
  • Your product is mostly made in the United States.

Probably not a fit

  • You sell only to US customers, including US-based importers.
  • Every foreign sale is prepaid or secured by a confirmed letter of credit.
  • You are a trader re-exporting goods mostly made outside the United States.
  • Your foreign buyers are consumers, not businesses.
  • You have more than 500 employees. We will refer you to a brokerage built for your size.

Why the small end has been underserved

There are roughly 270,000 small and mid-size exporters in the United States, about 65,000 of them manufacturers. Only a small fraction insure their foreign receivables. That is not because the coverage does not exist; it does, and it is designed for exactly these companies. It is because the brokers who place it earn too little on a small policy to go looking for small exporters.

We will go looking. Our firm is being built around tools that find, qualify and serve small exporters at a cost that makes the small policy worth writing. That is the whole idea.
