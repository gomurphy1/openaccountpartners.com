Insurance for the ninety days between shipped and paid.

Open Account Partners will arrange short-term export credit insurance for small and mid-size US exporters that sell to foreign buyers on open-account terms. If the buyer does not pay, the policy does.

Built for exporters the big brokers skip.

A $2 million exporter generates the same application, the same monthly reporting and the same renewal as a $200 million one. The large brokerages cannot make the math work at the small end, so they do not try.

We are building Open Account Partners to make the math work. Our back office will run on software that does the finding, qualifying and paperwork, so a small policy can be served as carefully as a large one.

How working with us will go.

Three steps, in this order. Most of the work happens on our side.

  1. A short conversation about your foreign sales.

    Who your buyers are, which countries, how much you ship on terms, and how long you wait to be paid. If you are not a fit, we will say so on that call.

  2. We prepare the application and place the policy.

    We will assemble the application, buyer information and supporting detail, and present a quote with the premium, coverage percentage and any buyer limits spelled out in plain terms.

  3. We handle the servicing for the life of the policy.

    Monthly shipment reporting, new-buyer approvals, claims if a buyer fails to pay, and renewal each year. You export; we keep the policy current.

Why insure a receivable at all?

Because an insured foreign receivable is worth more than an uninsured one, and not only on the day a buyer defaults.

Insured receivables can be borrowed against. Many lenders will advance on foreign invoices only when they are covered, so a policy can turn money you are waiting on into working capital you can use now.

Insurance also lets you say yes to buyers you would otherwise turn away, or offer terms to a customer who is asking for them. Competitors in Europe and Asia routinely sell on open account because their exporters routinely insure. US small exporters mostly do not, and lose orders over it.
